# Fix the dates before chasing coverage

As of 2026-10-21, no source gives Sandwich Index accurate, dated, per-store McChicken prices that it can cleanly republish with nothing required of the owner: each store's real price still lives only in the logged-in McDonald's app, on menu boards and on receipts. The most urgent problem is the data already live, because the 289 Dallas-area MenuPriceTracker prices labelled "checked 2026-10-12" come from a single scrape published around **2026-04-16** and never refreshed, so the label overstates their freshness by six months and re-dating them is the first build (about 1.5 to 2 developer-days, no owner work). The one genuinely new source is **Drive Thru Index**, which posts a dated daily pickup price, McChicken included, for about **13,700** US McDonald's and calls its figures "free to quote with attribution." It beats MenuPriceTracker on every test the project uses, yet it has no terms, licence, named operator or contact, is three weeks old, and takes its prices from the McDonald's app, whose terms forbid automated extraction, the same flaw that ruled out the owner's own login. No official McDonald's web surface shows a store's in-store or pickup price: the only logged-out page with prices is the DoorDash-run delivery storefront, and the web guest ordering that McDonald's revised terms anticipate is not live and would still bar republishing. The recommendation is to re-date MenuPriceTracker now, keep the nationwide MenuPriceTracker read stopped, run a one-off Drive Thru Index comparison on the 289 Dallas stores to measure April's drift, and then, if the owner accepts the provenance risk already accepted for MenuPriceTracker, replace it with attributed per-store Drive Thru Index quotes. In parallel, Android and iOS receipt shortcuts make the only clean exact source cheaper to feed, though they will add tens of stores a month, not thousands.

## MenuPriceTracker froze in mid-April while its date labels kept moving

MenuPriceTracker is an anonymous site. Its domain was registered on **2026-03-09** behind a GoDaddy privacy proxy ([RDAP](https://rdap.org/domain/menupricetracker.com)), the Wayback Machine first captured it on 2026-03-30 ([Wayback CDX](https://web.archive.org/cdx/search/cdx?url=menupricetracker.com&matchType=domain&output=json&fl=timestamp,original,statuscode&collapse=urlkey&limit=400)), and its About page names a "Senior Price Analyst" whom no search could find, alongside no company, address or legal entity ([About, archived 2026-10-11](https://web.archive.org/web/20261011011158/https://menupricetracker.com/about)). It describes itself in ways that cannot both be true: the homepage promises "Real prices pulled from the McDonald's app — not estimates" ([MenuPriceTracker home](https://menupricetracker.com/)), while its disclaimer says "Prices shown are estimates from aggregated public data and may not reflect current prices at any specific location" ([Disclaimer, archived 2026-10-11](https://web.archive.org/web/20261011011357/https://menupricetracker.com/disclaimer)). The project's licensing email bounced on 2026-10-12 (`docs/ENGINEERING.md`), and the owner has since ruled out outreach, so there is no licence and no route to one.

The numbers themselves look like a real per-store scrape. Store pages carry franchise-specific quirks that would be hard to fabricate: a **$1 McChicken** beside an ordinary $5.69 Big Mac in Grandville, Michigan ([Grandville store](https://menupricetracker.com/mcdonalds/michigan/grandville/2980-44th-street)) and beside a $1 cheeseburger in Lancaster, Ohio ([Lancaster store](https://menupricetracker.com/mcdonalds/ohio/lancaster/1451-e-main-st)); limited-time items priced store by store, such as a Derpy McFlurry at $4.29 in Coppell, $4.69 on Dallas's West Mockingbird Lane and $6.89 at the Darien I-95 plaza ([Dallas store](https://menupricetracker.com/mcdonalds/texas/dallas/1717-w-mockingbird-ln); [Darien store](https://menupricetracker.com/mcdonalds/connecticut/darien/rt-95-northbound-between-exit-12-13)); and realistic spread inside a metro, with 24 Fresno stores carrying 12 distinct Big Mac prices between $5.39 and $6.89 ([Fresno](https://menupricetracker.com/mcdonalds/california/fresno)). The 289 Dallas-area McChicken prices the site shows run from **$1.49 to $2.99, median $2.19**, all ending in 9 and clustered the way franchise groups price, with all five Richardson stores at $2.19 (`src/data/feeds/menupricetracker.json`). The best reading is that MenuPriceTracker scraped each store's app menu once.

Once is the problem. Every page read on 2026-10-21 carried a `dateModified` stamp generated 10 to 25 seconds before the request, which is why it always says "last verified October 2026" ([Coppell store](https://menupricetracker.com/mcdonalds/texas/coppell/813-macarthur-blvd)). Underneath, every state, city and store page carries `"datePublished":"2026-04-16"`, all 57 sitemaps have a `lastmod` of 2026-04-16 ([sitemap index](https://menupricetracker.com/sitemap-index.xml)), and the homepage still reads "Last Updated: April 16, 2026" ([MenuPriceTracker home](https://menupricetracker.com/)). The data agrees with those dates. All **64 Los Angeles store Big Mac prices** and all **474 California city rows** were identical on 2026-08-07 and 2026-10-21 ([LA, archived 2026-08-07](https://web.archive.org/web/20260807004205/https://menupricetracker.com/mcdonalds/california/los-angeles); [LA live](https://menupricetracker.com/mcdonalds/california/los-angeles)); 322 of 410 Texas city rows are unchanged since 2026-03-30, with most of the rest moving by one to three cents ([Texas, archived 2026-03-30](https://web.archive.org/web/20260330155430/https://menupricetracker.com/mcdonalds/texas); [Texas live](https://menupricetracker.com/mcdonalds/texas)); and every page still lists McDonald's x Netflix "KPop Demon Hunters" items that launched on 2026-03-31 "for a limited time" ([AOL](https://www.aol.com/lifestyle/mcdonalds-bringing-back-menu-item-180000627.html)). The national Big Mac average held at $5.90 to $5.91 from March to October while The Economist's US Big Mac price rose 1.6% between January and July 2026 ([Econlife](https://econlife.com/2026/07/big-mac-index-3/)). If each LA store had even a 10% chance of changing its Big Mac price in those 2.5 months, the odds that none of the 64 changed would be about 0.1%; the 10% is an illustrative assumption, not a measured rate. The project's own reads tell the same story, with Dallas 1717 W Mockingbird at $1.99 and Coppell at $2.49 unmoved since August (`research/price-sources.md`).

Store-level accuracy remains unmeasured, because no independent, dated McChicken price at a named store turned up anywhere for 2025 or 2026. The Big Mac checks that exist are reassuring at city level and inconclusive below it. Across 12 city and state pairs, MenuPriceTracker sits 0.7% to 12.6% above CashNetUSA's February 2025 app averages, a median of 6.2% that fits 14 months of price rises ([Time Out](https://www.timeout.com/usa/news/this-map-shows-where-you-can-find-the-cheapest-and-most-expensive-big-macs-in-america-050225)). Reuters' $6.89 Fresno store has a possible exact match on MenuPriceTracker ([GV Wire/Reuters](https://gvwire.com/2026/09/29/why-does-a-big-mac-cost-5-69-at-one-fresno-location-6-89-at-another/)), while at the Darien I-95 plaza MenuPriceTracker's $8.49 is $1.70 below The Takeout's $10.19 from May 2025, a figure with no stated method ([The Takeout](https://www.thetakeout.com/1854125/expensive-mcdonalds-america-connecticut-big-mac/)).

For the site, this means every MenuPriceTracker price is about **six months old** and predates the **Under $3 Menu**, which from 2026-04-21 put the McChicken under $3 at participating restaurants, with "Price… dependent on location" ([QSR Web](https://www.qsrweb.com/news/mcdonalds-adds-under-3-menu-4-breakfast-meal-starting-april-21/)). That makes MenuPriceTracker's $3-plus McChicken prices elsewhere in the country suspect, though not in Dallas, where every value is under $3. Prices may also have moved for a newer reason: McDonald's told investors on 2026-09-23 that its pricing engine gives "pricing recommendations down to the restaurant and item level," as quoted in the class-action complaint ([Complaint, N.D. Ill. 1:26-cv-12149](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.ilnd.509041/gov.uscourts.ilnd.509041.1.0.pdf)). Labelling these prices "checked 2026-10-12" repeats a mistake already spreading downstream. One coupon site presented the same data as a "September 2026 database" ([Grocery Coupon Guide](https://www.grocerycouponguide.com/articles/the-same-big-mac-costs-more-depending-on-where-you-live-we-found-the-biggest-price-gaps/)), and the GitHub mcchicken-index's 45-day staleness guard cannot catch it, because MenuPriceTracker's dates always read as current ([mcchicken-index METHODOLOGY](https://github.com/14039/mcchicken-index/blob/master/METHODOLOGY.md)). Resuming the paused nationwide read would carry that error to about 12,000 more stores.

## Drive Thru Index is the only new near-census source, quotable but unlicensed

Drive Thru Index (drivethruindex.com) registered its domain on **2026-09-30** with the registrant hidden ([Verisign RDAP](https://rdap.verisign.com/com/v1/domain/drivethruindex.com)). It describes "Daily pickup prices from every restaurant of the biggest fast food brands in 54 countries," "collected from each chain's own ordering app," and says the figures "are free to quote with attribution" in the form "Source: Drive Thru Index, prices checked 2026-10-21" ([llms.txt](https://drivethruindex.com/llms.txt)). Its public status log records US McDonald's runs pricing **13,737 stores on 2026-10-07 and 13,728 on 2026-10-21**, on a daily 08:00 UTC schedule ([status](https://drivethruindex.com/api/status)). A sample store page in Barre, Vermont lists 438 items "checked 2026-10-21," with the McChicken at **$2.39 for pickup and $3.99 for delivery** ([Barre store page](https://drivethruindex.com/restaurants/us/vt/barre/mcdonalds-12751)), and Dexerto covered the launch on 2026-10-12 ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/entertainment/website-tracks-fast-food-prices-every-day-to-help-you-find-cheapest-places-to-eat-3418130/)). Its downloadable CSVs hold only state, city and history aggregates, so there is no per-store file ([press page](https://drivethruindex.com/press)), and its robots.txt allows the HTML pages while disallowing `/api/` apart from the status endpoint ([robots.txt](https://drivethruindex.com/robots.txt)).

The weaknesses are serious. Requests for /about, /terms, /license, /contact and similar pages all returned 404 on 2026-10-21, no operator or email address appears anywhere, and its dataset metadata carries no licence ([home](https://drivethruindex.com/)). Its data comes from the McDonald's app, and McDonald's terms forbid using "any unauthorized technology or automated system to access the online services or extract content… including… screen scrapers" ([McDonald's USA Terms](https://www.mcdonalds.com/us/en-us/terms-and-conditions.html)), the same clause that made the owner's offered login unusable. Nobody has checked its accuracy independently, and a site this new can change its permission, block readers or vanish without notice.

The case for it is also real. If Sandwich Index quoted Drive Thru Index, it would never touch McDonald's services, so it would not be a party to the terms Drive Thru Index may be breaching. Prices are facts, which copyright does not protect ([Feist v. Rural, Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/499/340)). Drive Thru Index publishes no terms for a reader to accept, courts have declined to bind logged-off scrapers to terms they never agreed to ([Eric Goldman blog on Meta v. Bright Data](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2024/01/game-on-bright-data-scores-major-victory-in-web-scraping-dispute-with-meta-guest-blog-post.htm)), and the scrapers that lost on contract had expressly accepted terms, as hiQ did by creating accounts ([ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/alternative-data/hiq-v-linkedin-wrapped-up-web-scraping-lessons-learned/)). This is context, not legal advice. The grant is narrow, though. "Free to quote" covers quoting figures with credit, not copying a 13,700-store database every day, so it cannot serve as a licensed bulk feed. Fetching one store's figure when a visitor views that store, with Drive Thru Index's exact attribution line and a link, sits much closer to quoting than a nightly mirror does.

On the project's own tests Drive Thru Index dominates: the same upstream, plus a real date, a pickup-specific price and explicit, if thin, permission to quote. Neither pickup price has been checked against the counter, since no US study compares McDonald's app pickup prices with in-store prices. The consistency point cuts both ways. Anyone who accepts MenuPriceTracker on provenance grounds has no principled reason to reject Drive Thru Index, and anyone who rejects Drive Thru Index for scraping the app should drop MenuPriceTracker for the same reason.

Everything else new since 2026-10-12 came up empty for per-store prices. The class action filed on 2026-10-02 in the Northern District of Illinois contains no price tables, and its only named-store price is a 2023 "$18 Big Mac meal" in Darien ([Complaint](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.ilnd.509041/gov.uscourts.ilnd.509041.1.0.pdf)). Reuters' AI-pricing investigation published two app prices from unnamed company-run Fresno stores ([GV Wire/Reuters](https://gvwire.com/2026/09/29/why-does-a-big-mac-cost-5-69-at-one-fresno-location-6-89-at-another/)). New York's algorithmic-pricing law and the newer state surveillance-pricing laws require labels on personal-data pricing, not per-store price posting ([Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/new-yorks-algorithmic-pricing-disclosure-law-takes-effect)). Open Prices still holds **zero** McDonald's prices and has not shipped restaurant menu support ([Open Prices API](https://prices.openfoodfacts.org/api/v1/locations?osm_name__like=McDonald&size=5); [issue #1074](https://github.com/openfoodfacts/open-prices/issues/1074)). Google's Business Profile food menus have an item price field, but only a location's owner or manager can call that API ([Google](https://developers.google.com/my-business/reference/rest/v4/accounts.locations/getFoodMenus)); the Places API offers only price levels ([Google](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/places/web-service/data-fields)); and Yelp's API terms, updated 2026-09-22, ban storing content beyond 24 hours or building a database ([Yelp API Terms](https://terms.yelp.com/developers/api_terms/)). getfoodmenus.com publishes national figures only ([getfoodmenus](https://getfoodmenus.com/data)), and MenuPriced's small sample uses delivery menus ([MenuPriced](https://menupriced.com/mcdonalds-menu-prices/)). The two academic datasets with per-store McDonald's prices, Ashenfelter and Jurajda's phone survey and Sosinskiy and Reich's Uber Eats scrape, have no public data deposit and do not cover 2026 McChicken prices ([NBER w28506](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28506/w28506.pdf); [IRLE](https://irle.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/sosinskiy_reich_2025.pdf)).

## McDonald's own web surfaces show delivery prices or none

Checked by hand on 2026-10-21, McDonald's location pages show an address, hours and an app prompt but no dollar amounts ([Bethany Beach location page](https://www.mcdonalds.com/us/en-us/location/de/bethany-beach/1-addy-rd/7959.html)), and the McChicken page offers only the national "Under $3 Menu" claim with "*Prices and participation may vary. Excludes McDelivery" ([McChicken page](https://www.mcdonalds.com/us/en-us/product/mcchicken.html)). Every "Order Pickup" button opens an app download prompt, and Mobile Order & Pay requires "McDonald's app download and registration" ([Mobile Order & Pay](https://www.mcdonalds.com/us/en-us/mobile-order-and-pay.html)). The store-locator endpoint that All The Places reads returns locations and amenities, not menus ([All The Places spider](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alltheplaces/alltheplaces/master/locations/spiders/mcdonalds.py)).

The only logged-out page with a store's prices is the DoorDash-run storefront, and its prices are delivery prices. One Louisiana store showed a full menu without a login or address, with the McChicken at **$3.79** and a $2.99 delivery fee ([order.online store page](https://order.online/en-US/store/mcdonalds-25033111)), against a national promise of a McChicken under $3 that "Excludes McDelivery." McDonald's itself warns that web delivery prices "may be higher than at restaurants" ([McDelivery page](https://www.mcdonalds.com/us/en-us/mcdelivery.html)), and DoorDash's storefront terms bar copying "menu content, for republication in any format or media" and accessing the site "manually or with any robot… to… copy… or monitor" it ([DoorDash Online Ordering terms](https://help.doordash.com/en-us/consumers/online-ordering-consumer-terms-and-conditions)).

One development deserves watching. McDonald's US terms, effective 2026-08-24, now cover order-ahead "via the McDonald's mobile app or website," say "guest ordering without an account may be available on McDonald's website," and add "Each restaurant independently determines its own prices" ([McDonald's USA Terms](https://www.mcdonalds.com/us/en-us/terms-and-conditions.html); [ConductAtlas diff](https://conductatlas.com/diff/mcdonalds/mcdonalds-terms-of-use/1282/7625/)). No web pickup flow was visible on 2026-10-21, but if it launches it would be the first official surface to show a store's pickup price without a login. It would not become a lawful feed, because the same terms grant only a "personal, non-commercial" licence, define content to include "data compilations," and forbid commercial republishing, automated extraction and deep links. The owner's own login falls under exactly these terms, and using it would be express assent of the kind on which hiQ lost its contract claim ([ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/alternative-data/hiq-v-linkedin-wrapped-up-web-scraping-lessons-learned/)), which is why the offer was declined.

## Exact prices still arrive only through visitors, so make that moment cheaper

The only clean source of an exact, dated, per-store price is a visitor's own receipt or app screenshot, which the site already reads in the browser, and the useful builds lower the effort of contributing rather than create contributors. An **Android share target** lets an installed web app receive a photo straight from the gallery or the McDonald's app, with the service worker keeping the image on the device for the existing reader. It works in Chrome for Android, Edge and Samsung Internet but not in Safari or Firefox ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Progressive_web_apps/Manifest/Reference/share_target); [browser-compat-data](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/blob/main/manifests/webapp/share_target.json)), Android is 41.6% of US mobile use ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/os-market-share/mobile/united-states-of-america)), and it takes about 1 to 2 days with nothing from the owner. An **iOS Shortcut** using Apple's "Extract Text from Image" action can cover the 58.4% of US phones on iOS by sending only the extracted text by POST ([Apple Shortcuts guide](https://support.apple.com/en-al/guide/shortcuts/apd58d46713f/ios); [action reference](https://matthewcassinelli.com/actions/extract-text-from-image/)); it takes about a day plus a text endpoint, although someone must publish its iCloud link once. Contributor badges and "unpriced stores near you" prompts add a small, unmeasured lift for 1 to 2 days of work, and a review of 110 papers found gamification generally raises crowdsourcing participation ([Morschheuser et al.](https://research.utu.fi/converis/portal/detail/Publication/26304743?lang=en_US)). An opt-in AI reader for unreadable photos would cost about **$0.0006 per image** on Claude Haiku 5.5 ([Claude pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing); [vision docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)), but it uploads the photo, breaking the site's no-upload promise for those cases, and needs an API key and spend cap set up once, which makes it an owner step. An Open Prices watcher costs half a day and will yield nothing until that project adds restaurant menus.

Expect modest yield. The research could not measure site traffic, and its rough judgement is tens of stores a month at low traffic, not thousands. For scale, Open Prices, a well-known open project with its own app, grew from about 150,000 prices in early 2026 to 333,239 by October across every product and country it covers ([FOSDEM 2026 slides](https://fosdem.org/2026/events/attachments/CNPVJL-lightning_lightning_talks_1/slides/267016/open_pric_eojra1o.pdf); [Open Prices API](https://prices.openfoodfacts.org/api/v1/prices?size=1)). Those same receipts are also the only true accuracy test for any third-party price. Pairing each receipt with the MenuPriceTracker or Drive Thru Index price for that store would pin the exact-match rate to within 15 points after about 43 pairs and within 10 points after about 96 (standard sample-size arithmetic at 95% confidence), and a rule fixed in advance, such as greying out a source if fewer than half of the first 30 pairs match exactly or the median error exceeds $0.30, keeps the decision honest. That report is about half a day of work.

Everything else examined should not be built. Street imagery is open but useless: in a random sample of 150 US stores, only **4 had a Panoramax photo from 2025 or later** within about 78 metres, taken 32 to 80 metres away where menu digits are a few pixels tall ([Panoramax API](https://api.panoramax.xyz/api/search?bbox=-125,24,-66,49.5&limit=3)), and Mapillary needs an account token and bars unapproved data mining ([Mapillary terms](https://www.mapillary.com/terms)). Flickr and Openverse photos are old and mostly non-US, and Openverse's robots.txt blocks its image API ([Openverse robots.txt](https://api.openverse.org/robots.txt)). Reddit has required approval for new API tokens since November 2025, and its terms restrict deriving data from posts ([Reddit Data API Terms](https://redditinc.com/policies/data-api-terms)). SMS needs carrier registration under the owner's identity ([Twilio A2P 10DLC](https://www.twilio.com/docs/trust-hub/registrations/a2p-10dlc-brand)), chat bots need human-created accounts, and a browser extension would see only DoorDash delivery prices. Emailed receipts wait on confirming that US McDonald's sends itemised e-receipts, which its UK app does ([UK app FAQ](https://www.mcdonalds.com/gb/en-gb/good-to-know/in-our-restaurants/app-faq.html)) but no US source confirms. A commercial phone survey, the method Ashenfelter and Jurajda used across McDonald's US restaurants, is lawful but needs the owner's money ([NBER w28506](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28506/w28506.pdf)).

Do rank 1 now, because the live site currently states something untrue. Do rank 2 next: comparing Drive Thru Index's daily pickup price with MenuPriceTracker's April figure at the 289 Dallas stores is the only way to put a number on April's drift without receipts, and it tests whether Drive Thru Index reads the same per-store menus. Because both come from the app, agreement supports both and disagreement marks prices that changed after April or an error in one of them, so it is a consistency check rather than ground truth. A sensible pre-set rule is that if fewer than half the Dallas stores match exactly, MenuPriceTracker prices come off display rather than merely being re-dated. Rank 3 then rests on the owner's yes or no, which is a decision rather than a chore. The recommendation is yes, on condition that each figure is fetched per store on demand, carries Drive Thru Index's attribution line and check date, links to the source page, is labelled as an app pickup price distinct from a receipt, and disappears rather than falling back to stale data when Drive Thru Index is unavailable. Matching its pages to the site's stores is the open engineering question; if the number in its URLs, such as `mcdonalds-12751`, proves to be McDonald's own restaurant number, the site's existing All The Places crosswalk would make the match exact, but this is unverified. In every scenario, keep the nationwide MenuPriceTracker read stopped: it would spend about a day of reading to put April prices on roughly 12,000 stores, and Drive Thru Index, if accepted, covers the same stores with today's date.

## Conclusion

The question has shifted from finding coverage to being honest about time. Two anonymous sites now claim near-census per-store McDonald's prices, both built on the app and registered seven months apart, and neither can grant a clean licence, because their data rests on collection McDonald's terms forbid. That makes the real choice binary: either Sandwich Index shows third-party app scrapes, in which case the fresh, dated, quotable one should replace the stale, mislabelled one, or it shows none and becomes a receipts-only index whose coverage grows slowly but whose every number is clean. Either is defensible; a six-month-old snapshot labelled as checked this month is not.

Drive Thru Index's most valuable use may be as a yardstick rather than a source. Even if the owner never displays it, a periodic comparison turns the question this project has asked since August, whether MenuPriceTracker is still current, into a measured drift rate, and the same comparison against incoming receipts would for the first time put an accuracy number on both scrapers. The one change that could reset the field is McDonald's launching web guest ordering, which would let anyone check a store's pickup price without an account. It would make spot-checks easy but would not make republishing lawful, because the terms that would govern it already forbid exactly that.
